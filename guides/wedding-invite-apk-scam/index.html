The wedding card that empties your account: the fake invite APK scam

It's wedding season, and the invitations are arriving on WhatsApp. Some of them aren't cards at all. They're apps that, once you let them, can read your OTPs and use your bank account while you watch the screen. Here's how to tell, and what to do if you've already tapped install.

The short answer

A real wedding card is a PDF or a JPG image, never an app. If the file ends in .apk, it's an Android app installer, and police across India warn it's a scam. Don't open it, even if it came from a relative. If you've already installed it: turn off mobile data and Wi-Fi, call 1930, and ask your bank to block your accounts, from another phone if you can.

The scam in one paragraph

A message arrives on WhatsApp with warm wishes, a couple's names and a file to “view the invitation”. Often it comes from a relative's or friend's number, because their phone was already hacked the same way and is now sending the file to everyone they know. The file isn't a card. Its name ends in .apk, which means it's an Android app installer. Tap it, follow the prompts, and you've installed software that can read your messages and work your phone for someone else. Rajasthan Police have warned of files named “invitation.apk” that can get at SMS, contact lists, the camera and stored files (PTI, November 2025).

The three permissions it needs

The app can't do much on its own. It needs you to say yes three times, and each yes unlocks more:

Permission it asks forWhat it unlocks
1. Install unknown appsLets WhatsApp, your browser or file manager install an app that didn't come from the Play Store. Without it, the “card” can't install at all.
2. AccessibilityMeant for people who need help using a phone. Abused, it lets the app read everything on your screen, watch what you type, tap buttons for you, approve its own permissions, and block you from uninstalling it.
3. SMSLets it read your messages, including the OTPs your bank sends, so it can approve payments without you seeing them.

The Home Ministry's Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) calls this kind of malware “Android God Mode”: once it has Accessibility, it can block or get around attempts to revoke its permissions or uninstall it (ETV Bharat, 31 March 2026).

How it plays out

A made-up example, pieced together from police advisories: Suresh, 58, a bank clerk in Pune, gets “Rahul weds Priya – invitation” from his cousin's number. He taps it. His phone asks to allow installs from WhatsApp; he allows it. The “card” asks for Accessibility “to open the invitation”; he allows it. It asks for SMS; it's allowed. A blank card appears and he thinks no more of it. That night, while the phone sits on the charger, the app opens his UPI app, enters payments, reads the OTPs and deletes the bank's messages. By morning his savings account is empty, and the same “card” has gone to everyone in his contacts.

That's not far from real cases. In Bengaluru, a 42-year-old businessman reportedly lost ₹5,00,440 after opening a fake wedding-invite APK, in several transactions within minutes (LatestLY, May 2026). In Hyderabad, the Cyber Crime Police reported a major financial loss for an IT employee who opened an APK “invitation”, and warned that genuine invitations come as PDF or JPG files and any request to download an APK should be treated with suspicion (ETV Bharat, 25 May 2026).

The same trick in other disguises

The wedding card is just one costume. The same kind of APK arrives as:

  • RTO e-challan: “Your vehicle challan has been generated”, with a file like “RTO Challan.apk”. CERT-In, the government's cyber security agency, warned that these apps read SMS and capture OTPs (alert of March 2026). Check challans only at echallan.parivahan.gov.in, typed in yourself.
  • Pensioners' life certificate (Jeevan Pramaan): I4C lists fake Jeevan Pramaan, SBI YONO, RTO challan and customer support apps among the disguises.
  • Bank or KYC apps: “Your account will be blocked, update KYC” with an app attached. Banks don't send apps on WhatsApp.
  • “8th Pay Commission” salary calculators: sent to government employees and pensioners. I4C has said plainly that the government does not send APK files on WhatsApp (India TV, 14 February 2026).

The government never sends APK files on WhatsApp. Neither do banks, the RTO or pension offices.

Three rules

  1. A real card is a PDF or JPG, never an app. If the name ends in .apk, delete it.
  2. Never allow “install unknown apps”, Accessibility or SMS access for anything that came in a chat, a text or a link.
  3. Call the sender on the number you already have, not a number in the message. If their phone was hacked, they'll want to know.

Already installed it?

Do these in order. Speed matters: the sooner your bank knows, the better the chance of stopping the money.

  1. Turn off mobile data and Wi-Fi. Not flight mode: you still want calls to work, so the bank and the police can reach you. Cutting the internet stops the app talking to the scammers.
  2. Call 1930 or report at cybercrime.gov.in, and call your bank's fraud line to block your accounts and cards. Use another phone if you can.
  3. Uninstall the app. If it won't let you, restart the phone in Safe Mode first: I4C calls Safe Mode the most effective way to get around this malware's blocks, because it switches off apps you installed. Then remove it from Settings → Apps. (How to start Safe Mode varies by phone; usually you press and hold the power-off option.)
  4. Change your banking passwords and UPI PIN from a safe device, not the infected phone.
  5. Warn your family and groups not to open anything that came from your number.
  6. Report the message on Chakshu (Sanchar Saathi), for messages received in the last 30 days.

If the app still won't go, or you're not sure the phone is clean, get it checked or reset it to factory settings after backing up photos and contacts.

Checklist to save or print

Fake wedding card? Three rules

  1. A real card is a PDF or JPG, never an .apk app.
  2. Never allow “install unknown apps”, Accessibility or SMS access for anything from a chat.
  3. Call the sender on the number you already have.

Already installed it? In this order

  1. Turn off mobile data and Wi-Fi (not flight mode).
  2. Call 1930 or use cybercrime.gov.in; ask your bank to block accounts. Use another phone if you can.
  3. Uninstall the app; if it won't go, restart in Safe Mode first.
  4. Change banking passwords and UPI PIN from a safe device.
  5. Warn your family and groups.
  6. Report the message on Chakshu: sancharsaathi.gov.in/sfc (within 30 days).

Google Play Protect: a safety net, not a guarantee

In India, Google Play Protect tries to block apps installed from browsers, messaging apps or file managers if they ask for permissions to read SMS, read notifications or use Accessibility, the ones fraudsters abuse most (Google, 3 October 2024). That catches a lot, but not everything, and scammers keep changing their apps. Anyone who asks you to turn Play Protect off is the scammer.

How big the problem is

  • 24,02,579 financial fraud complaints were filed on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal in 2025, reporting ₹22,495 crore lost (Ministry of Home Affairs, written reply in the Rajya Sabha, 11 February 2026).
  • Fast reporting works: the 1930 system has saved more than ₹11,158 crore in more than 32.80 lakh complaints, up to 30 June 2026 (PIB, July 2026).
