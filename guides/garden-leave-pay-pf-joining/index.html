Put on garden leave? Your pay, PF and when you can join elsewhere

Some companies cutting jobs tell people to stop working at once but keep paying them until the notice period ends. That's garden leave. You're still an employee until your last day, and that one fact decides your pay, your PF, your gratuity and when you can start somewhere else.

The short answer

On garden leave you're still employed: you get your salary, PF keeps going in, and the time counts towards gratuity, until the last working day. You can't join another employer before that day unless the company releases you early, and an early release can cost you gratuity if you're near 5 years. After your last day, a clause stopping you from working for a competitor generally isn't enforceable in India. Severance is taxed at your slab; gratuity up to ₹20 lakh is tax-free.

What garden leave is

  • Paid notice, at home. You stay on the payroll for the notice period but aren't given work, and usually lose system access.
  • It comes from your contract, not from a law. Check the notice and garden leave clauses in your appointment letter.
  • You're still bound by your contract: confidentiality, no working for anyone else, and returning laptops and data.
  • It can't run past your notice period. Severance, if offered, is a separate payment on top.

What still counts

SalaryPaid as usual, with TDS

Gratuity serviceCounts, up to the last working day. 5 years of continuous service are needed
LeaveUsually keeps accruing; check the policy. Encashment in the settlement

Karan's numbers

Karan, 34, in Bengaluru, joined on 15 February 2022. His fixed pay is ₹20 lakh a year (₹1,66,667 a month) and his basic is ₹1 lakh a month. On 1 December 2026, 4 years and 9½ months in, he's told his role is gone: garden leave to 31 January 2027, plus severance of 3 months' fixed pay.

Gratuity = 15/26 × ₹1,00,000 × 5 years = ₹2,88,462. Two weeks more on the payroll are worth almost ₹3 lakh, so it's worth asking HR for the later date in writing. Check your own dates with the gratuity calculator and the six-month rule.

Can you join a new job early?

  • Not while you're on garden leave. You're still employed; joining elsewhere breaks your contract, and your PF account would show two employers paying at once.
  • Ask for an early release. Employers sometimes agree, with a relieving letter dated earlier. Pay for the remaining garden leave then usually stops, and check the date against the 5-year mark first.
  • After your last day, a clause stopping you from joining a competitor is generally void under section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872. The Supreme Court has upheld restraints during employment (Niranjan Shankar Golikari, 1967), and courts have struck down post-employment non-competes. Confidentiality and non-solicitation clauses can still be enforced.

Leaving by choice rather than being let go? Compare buying out your notice.

How the money is taxed

  • Garden leave pay: ordinary salary, with TDS as usual.
  • Severance or ex gratia: "profits in lieu of salary" under section 18 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, taxed at your slab in the year you receive it. Karan's ₹5 lakh, on top of ₹16.67 lakh of salary this tax year, adds about ₹1.08 lakh of tax in the new regime.
  • Gratuity: tax-free up to ₹20 lakh for private-sector employees.
  • Leave encashment: tax-free within limits, at most ₹25 lakh over your career (see leave encashment tax).

Judging a package as a whole? Read how to judge an exit or severance offer, and check the settlement with the full and final settlement calculator.

What to ask for

  1. Your last working day in writing, and whether it can move past your 5-year date if you're close.
  2. Bonus: whether a pro-rata bonus for the year is paid (see bonus when you leave).
  3. Stock: which date the plan treats as your leaving date, and whether grants vesting during garden leave will vest (see RSUs and your last day).
  4. Health cover: until when, and ask the insurer to migrate you to an individual policy before it ends.
  5. Settlement timeline: final wages are due within 2 working days of your last day under the Code on Wages; read full and final settlement explained.

Sources

Garden leave is contractual in India; no statute governs it. Indian Contract Act, 1872, section 27; Niranjan Shankar Golikari v. Century Spinning and Mfg. Co.
