Car or bike insurance: the new four-cover format from 15 December

From 15 December, every car and two-wheeler policy in India comes in the same four-part format, with a price against each part. Two parts are compulsory; you choose the rest. Here's what each covers and how to pick.

By Salarist

Published 26 Nov 2026Car Ownership Cost4 min read

Verified on 26 November 2026. Applies to private cars and two-wheelers, for policies issued from 15 December 2026.

The short answer

Motor policies will be split into four covers: third-party liability and the owner's personal accident cover are compulsory; cover for passengers and for damage to your own vehicle are optional. You tick what you want on a signed customer option form, and the schedule shows the premium for each cover. It applies to every policy issued from 15 December 2026, new or renewed. A running policy stays as it is. The main new decision is whether to add the passenger cover.

The four covers

CoverWhat it pays forMust you take it?
I. Third-party liabilityInjury, death or property damage you cause to othersYes, by law
II. Legal liability to occupantsClaims by passengers, a pillion rider or your driver who are hurt or killed, up to the seating capacity. Not your spouse, children or parentsOptional
III. Personal accidentOwner: the full sum for death or permanent total disability, half for loss of one limb or eye. Passengers and other drivers: optional add-onOwner's cover: yes (unless you already have accident cover from another policy). Passengers: optional
IV. Own damageDamage to or theft of your vehicleOptional

Family members travelling with you aren't covered under Section II; personal accident cover for passengers (Section III) is the way to cover them. The owner's accident cover is only for individual owners and only on one vehicle if you own several.

Choosing for a family car

Neha, 34, is buying a ₹10 lakh hatchback in December on a car loan. She drives her husband and parents around most weekends; a part-time driver takes her father to the doctor twice a week.

CoverNeha's choiceWhy
I. Third-partyYesCompulsory
II. OccupantsYesCovers the paid driver and any friend she gives a lift to
III. Owner's accident coverYesCompulsory; she has no other accident cover
III. Passenger accident coverYesThe only way to cover her family in the car
IV. Own damageYesA new car on a loan: lenders usually insist on it, and a repair bill would hurt

Her cousin rides a 9-year-old scooter worth about ₹20,000 and always rides alone. For him, the two compulsory covers may be enough: own-damage cover on a vehicle worth so little may not be worth its premium. Each person ticks what fits; the form makes the choice visible.

Renew before or after 15 December?

  • Policy running? Nothing to do. You don't need to cancel it. If you want passenger liability cover (Section II) before renewal, you can add it mid-term for an extra premium, from the insurer that issued your third-party cover.
  • Renewal due in December? Renew on time, before or after the 15th. Don't let it lapse to wait for the new format: driving without third-party cover is an offence. A renewal from 15 December will come in the new format with the option form.
  • Price: the circular lets insurers price own-damage cover freely, so compare quotes; seeing each cover's premium separately makes that easier.

Buying a car this December

If the car is delivered on or after 15 December, the dealer's policy will come with the option form. Read it before you sign, and check that only the covers you want are ticked. You can buy from another insurer if the dealer's quote is high, as long as the policy is in place before the car is registered. Weighing a December purchase at all? Read Buy a car in December or wait for January? and see the full cost with the car ownership cost calculator.

Before you pay

  1. The option form: every cover ticked or unticked as you meant, signed (or OTP-approved online). No policy can be issued without it.
  2. The Customer Information Sheet: what's covered and excluded, in plain words.
  3. Premium per cover on the schedule, and the insured declared value (IDV) for own damage.
  4. Owner's accident cover: skip it only if you have accident cover from another policy, and keep proof.

Paying for the car too: compare car loan or cash and new or used.

Sources

Thungala Dhana Laxmi (4 August 2026); summarised by Cafemutual, Moneylife, TaxGuru and Business Standard (November 2026). Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 (compulsory third-party cover).
